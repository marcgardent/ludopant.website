Base Node VS Cinema 4D MoGraph

Cinema 4D (MoGraph), by Maxon. Cinema 4D is a 3D application; MoGraph is its procedural motion design toolset of cloners, effectors and fields. It is sold by subscription, alone or in Maxon One.[1,2]

Base Node is a text language for Blender's node trees, with a free add-on and paid packs of tested node groups. MoGraph lives in a separate 3D application, with objects, tags and a fields list you set in panels. Base Node follows the same model in Blender's Geometry Nodes: a cloner pipes into effectors, each weighed by falloffs, as in Cube() |> grid_cloner() |> random_effector(). The pack Motion Graphics holds the cloners, effectors and falloffs.

Of the 6 capabilities below, Base Node covers 2, covers 3 in part and does not cover 1. The Cinema 4D MoGraph side of each row was checked on Maxon's pages on 2026-11-20; the numbers link to them.

Where Cinema 4D MoGraph is ahead

  • The Sound Effector and Sound Field drive clones from frequency ranges of an audio file.[10,11]
  • Voronoi Fracture splits a mesh from points of polygons, splines, particles or textures.[12]
  • One Dynamics Body tag turns every clone into a rigid body.[9]
  • Fields stack as layers in a list, with modifier layers such as Decay, Quantize and Freeze.[8]
  • Redshift comes with every Cinema 4D subscription.[1]

When to choose which

  • Choose Cinema 4D MoGraph when

    You want a mature motion design application: Fields layers, Sound and Shader effectors, Voronoi Fracture, MoGraph dynamics and Redshift in one place.

  • Choose Base Node when

    You already work in Blender and want the cloner and effector workflow there, as Geometry Nodes you can read, version and render with Cycles or EEVEE.

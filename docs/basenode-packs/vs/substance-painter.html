Base Node VS Substance Painter

Adobe Substance 3D Painter, by Adobe. A 3D painting application that puts textures, materials and masks on meshes. It is sold by subscription in the Substance 3D Texturing and Collection plans; a Steam edition also exists.[1,2]

Base Node is a text language for Blender's node trees, with a free add-on and paid packs of tested node groups. Substance Painter is a separate application: you bake mesh maps, paint and stack layers, then export texture sets. Base Node does the procedural half inside Blender, with no bake and no export: Material Layers stacks materials by masks in shader nodes, and Smart Materials computes the mesh maps live and turns them into masks and wear. There are no brushes.

Of the 7 capabilities below, Base Node covers 2, covers 3 in part and does not cover 2. The Substance Painter side of each row was checked on Adobe's pages on 2026-11-20; the numbers link to them.

Where Substance Painter is ahead

  • You paint on the mesh with brushes, through a stencil aligned to the screen.[11]
  • Particle brushes throw paint that collides with the mesh.[10]
  • UV Tiles projects give each UDIM tile its own set of textures.[14]
  • It bakes mesh maps from several high-poly meshes through a cage.[4]
  • Export presets write packed texture sets for Unity and Unreal Engine.[12]

When to choose which

  • Choose Substance Painter when

    You paint by hand: brushes, stencils, projection, particle brushes, UDIM tiles, texture sets exported to a game engine.

  • Choose Base Node when

    Your models are rendered in Blender and the wear follows the mesh: layered materials, edge wear and dirt that update when the model changes, as node groups you can read and version.

Base Node VS Nuke

Nuke, NukeX, Nuke Studio, by Foundry. Node-based compositing software for film and episodic work. NukeX adds camera tracking, clean-up and machine-learning tools, and Nuke Studio adds a timeline. All are commercial; Nuke Non-commercial is free for personal use, limited to HD output.[1,2]

Base Node is a text language for Blender's node trees, with a free add-on and paid packs of tested node groups. Nuke is a separate compositing application with its own viewer, roto tools and pipeline. Base Node takes the other road: its modules are node groups for Blender's own compositor, written as .bn text, so the shot is rendered and finished in one file. Compositing Pro follows Nuke's keying, light, grain, render-pass and 360° tools; Motion Compositing adds the transitions and stylize effects Nuke leaves to other tools.

Of the 14 capabilities below, Base Node covers 7, covers 5 in part and does not cover 2. The Nuke side of each row was checked on Foundry's pages on 2026-11-20; the numbers link to them.

Where Nuke is ahead

  • RotoPaint animates spline shapes and paints, clones and reveals pixels in the Viewer.[26]
  • Deep compositing moves elements in depth without a new render.[32]
  • CameraTracker (NukeX) solves the camera of live footage into an animated 3D camera.[34]
  • CopyCat (NukeX) trains a neural network on a few frames and applies the effect to the whole shot.[35]
  • Kronos and Smart Vector use optical flow to retime footage and carry paint across frames.[31,36]
  • Nuke Studio adds a timeline for conform, editorial, playback and export.[1]

When to choose which

  • Choose Nuke when

    You composite live-action plates: roto, paint, camera tracking, deep images, optical-flow retiming and a studio pipeline that already speaks Nuke.

  • Choose Base Node when

    Your shots are rendered in Blender and you want Nuke's keyer, grade, glow, grain and pass tools in Blender's compositor, in a file you can read and diff, without a second application.
